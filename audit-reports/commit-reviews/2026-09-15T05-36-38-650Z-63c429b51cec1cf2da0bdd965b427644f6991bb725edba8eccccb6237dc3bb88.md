When `recovered` is false, execution falls through to returning `{ envelope: errorEnvelope("specPatch", requestId, "INTERNAL_ERROR", "git push failed after reconcile", { retryable: true, causeCode: "GIT_PUSH_FAILED" }) }`.
  - **Expected behavior**: If `pushBotCommits` rejects due to `FOREIGN_COMMITS` during post-reconcile push retry, the refusal must be preserved and surfaced as a non-retryable error with `causeCode: "FOREIGN_COMMITS"` and `retryable: false`, identical to the primary push failure handling at lines 127–136.
  - **Trigger scenario**: A push is initially rejected because the remote moved (e.g. concurrent push or remote divergence). The recovery block runs `reconcileClone` to fetch and rebase onto `origin/${branch}`. During the subsequent `pushBotCommits`, `foreignAuthors` detects a commit authored by someone other than `identity.email` (e.g. introduced by rebase or present in the clone). `pushBotCommits` throws a refusal with `causeCode = "FOREIGN_COMMITS"`. The `.catch((retryError) => false)` handler swallows the refusal, setting `recovered = false` and returning `retryable: true` with `causeCode: "GIT_PUSH_FAILED"`.
  - **Impact**: Upstream callers receive a response claiming the push failure is transient and retryable (`retryable: true`, `causeCode: "GIT_PUSH_FAILED"`). Clients will repeatedly retry pushing an unpublishable commit rather than failing fast on policy refusal, masking a security/authorship violation behind a network/concurrency retry loop.
